# Editorial policy foundation

Outsaid serves the public interest and applies the same evidentiary standard across parties, institutions, communities and ideologies. Delhi NCR is the initial editorial focus.

1. Every submitted report remains private until a human reviewer approves publication. No popularity threshold, automated score or AI output is a substitute for review.
2. Publication status and factual verification are independent. A published report is not automatically corroborated. Label allegations, eyewitness accounts and documented information clearly; a document may itself contain an unverified claim.
3. Seek original sources, assess provenance and context, and attribute claims precisely. Never invent quotations, sources, witnesses or evidence. Avoid sensational headlines and unsupported certainty.
4. Review evidence for personal data, vulnerable people and retaliation risk. Do not expose private contributor identities, witness details or real-time locations of at-risk people. Collect only necessary broad locality information.
5. Do not promise anonymity without a technical and operational guarantee. Explain what information the service retains before contributions open.
6. Use an explicit public DTO and a human-reviewed revision. Private notes and source snapshots remain private; publishing a report never grants blanket permission to release its evidence.
7. Record moderation decisions, reasons, revisions, corrections and withdrawals. Corrections should be visible and understandable; do not silently rewrite published claims.
8. Provide a human process for accuracy/privacy complaints and a chance to respond where appropriate. Flags are review signals, not factual verdicts.

This document establishes implementation requirements, not an operational claim that a staffed newsroom or moderation service is already available. Review procedures, retention periods and correction/appeal workflows must be finalized before public submissions.
